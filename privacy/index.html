NodNod — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 27 November 2026

NodNod is an iPhone app made by Spyridon Pikoulas. It works on your device: it has no account and no servers of its own, and nothing you put in it reaches us unless it is emailed to us. This policy says what it keeps and where.

What NodNod keeps

On your iPhone it keeps only what it needs to work:

  • the stops you save and the last five you went to: their names, where they are, the kind of place, and the alarm sound if you picked one for a trip there
  • your routines: their names, stops, days, times, when to ring and the alarm sound if you picked one, and the day each last started or was skipped; a routine’s name and stop show in its notifications
  • the trip you chose for one-press start: its stops, when to ring and the alarm sound if you picked one
  • during a trip, until it ends: its stops and alarms, when it started and rang, your last position, and how far you have travelled and have left; the stop and the distance left can show on the Lock Screen
  • the last trip’s summary (its stop, end time, minutes and distances), until you close it or start another
  • your settings, the last trip’s alarm sound and where you left the report-a-bug button
  • a bug report you wrote, on the clipboard, if there is no mail app to send it with

None of it leaves your iPhone, other than in your iPhone’s own backups (iCloud or your computer, if you use them), what you choose to share, and what it sends to Apple Maps, below. Deleting NodNod deletes its data from the iPhone.

The report-a-bug button writes an email to us, which goes only if you send it, from your own mail account. It carries what you wrote, the versions of NodNod and iOS, your iPhone’s model and language, and, if it is sent with Apple’s Mail and you leave it attached, a picture of the screen you were on, which can show where you are on the map, your stops and your routines. We use it only to answer you and to fix or improve NodNod.

To find stops and draw the map, NodNod sends what you type in search, as you type it, and the places it suggests, the area around you that search favours (50 km across, centred on your last known position, if Location is on), the part of the map you look at and, while Pick on Map is open, the pin’s point and the parts of the map around it to Apple Maps; see Apple Maps’ privacy policy.

Pick on Map opens on the stop you are moving, with the route’s other stops in view. When you add a stop to a route that has others, it opens on those, and on your last known position too if that is within 200 km of one of them. Otherwise it opens where you are, if Location is on. Each time the map comes to rest, Apple Maps gets the exact point under the pin, to find its address (unless the pin is on a stop you chose there). NodNod also asks Apple Maps which stops are in the part of the map you see. It does this by the squares of a fixed grid, sending the middle of each and how far around it to look: the 400 m square under the pin, every 400 m square in view when you are zoomed in close, and squares of about 3 to 100 km for stations, until you zoom far out. It skips squares already answered while the map is open and asks for at most 30 squares a minute. When it opens where you are, or you move it there, the point and squares show Apple Maps where you are. When you close Pick on Map, NodNod keeps none of this but the stop you picked.

NodNod asks for these permissions with its reason first; you can change your answer in Settings at any time:

  • Location, to search near you, to show where you are on the map before and during a trip, and to know how far your stop is during one, also while NodNod is in the background or your iPhone is locked; and, set to Always, to start a routine’s trip on its own (NodNod Pro), as described after this list; without it, NodNod cannot tell when you near your stop.
  • Alarms, to ring like an alarm clock when you near your stop, even on silent; without it, NodNod cannot ring through silent mode or a Focus.
  • Notifications, to tell you at a routine’s time on its days that NodNod will follow its trip once you set off (without Always, that opening NodNod starts it), with Not Today to skip it that day, and to say when that trip has started on its own, if you set up a routine (NodNod Pro); they are scheduled on your iPhone, and no server sends them; without it, routines still start, without telling you.

With Location set to Always and a routine saved, NodNod asks iOS to wake it in the background each time your iPhone has moved some way, usually 500 m or more, and no more often than every few minutes. This goes on all day, every day, for as long as you have a routine and NodNod Pro, not only around its times. When woken, NodNod does not use where you are, only the time: unless a routine is due (on one of its days, up to 45 minutes after its time, not yet started or skipped that day, and with NodNod Pro), it does nothing and keeps nothing. When one is due (when NodNod wakes or you open it), its trip starts wherever you are and is followed like a trip you start yourself, until it rings at your stop, you end it or tap Not Today, or 3 hours pass without it ringing. Deleting your last routine, changing Location from Always or NodNod Pro ending stops the waking; so does closing NodNod from the app switcher, until you open it again.

Purchases

NodNod Pro is sold through Apple’s App Store. Apple handles the payment and tells the app only whether a purchase is active; we never see your name, Apple Account or payment details. Apple’s handling of that data is in Apple’s privacy policy.

Analytics and sharing

NodNod has no analytics, advertising or tracking, and shares no data with anyone beyond what this policy says. Apple gives us App Store figures such as downloads and sales in aggregate, and crash reports only from people who choose to share them with developers in their iPhone’s settings; none of it identifies you.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date above changes with it.
